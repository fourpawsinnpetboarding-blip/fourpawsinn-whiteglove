# Hook rules (first frame and first line)

Source: Devin Jatho's teardown of a Hormozi reel (instagram.com/p/Dc24oGkA-Ip), sent by Alex Oct 8, 2026. Applies to every Four Paws Inn reel, static, and carousel cover from Claude and Codex.

## Visual hook (the first frame)
1. Six elements or fewer. The brain reads about six things at once. Count every text box, badge, logo, face, and dog.
2. One headline, five words or fewer when possible.
3. One proof element. For Four Paws Inn: the review badge (4.9, five stars, 215 Google reviews). Text reels: set `"proof": true` on clip 1 in the spec.
4. The dog is the subject. Fill the frame. No empty yard openers.
5. Cut anything that is not needed to understand the hook (dates, extra stats, small print).

## Verbal hook (the first line, spoken or on screen)
1. Talk to the viewer, not about us. "Your dog" beats "our dogs".
2. Promise value or a payoff: "here are 3 things to pack", "watch what happens at 7 AM".
3. Pattern: "[Result or scene] for your dog. Here is [the payoff]."

## Examples
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Sunday here looks like this | Your dog's Sunday here looks like this |
| Never board your dog sight unseen | Meet us before you book. Here is how |
| Play time with friends | Where your dog plays while you travel |

## QA check (add to every reel review)
Count first frame elements (6 max), confirm the proof badge, confirm the first line says "you" or "your".

## Personal brand (Alex's own content)
Same rules. The proof element changes: a real number on screen (monthly revenue, dogs per night, bookings this week), shown as a clean dashboard or bank screenshot, never a cluttered spreadsheet. First line speaks to the pet business owner: "You run a boarding business. Here is the number that matters."
