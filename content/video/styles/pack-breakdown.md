# Style: "Pack breakdown" reels (Alex, Oct 30, 2026)

Reference Alex picked: @packleaderdogs (Steve Del Savio), https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcUO8DQPsZS/
715K views, 40.5K likes, 279 comments, 96 seconds.

## What the reference does
1. Opens on the moment of tension. First words point at it: "Look at the difference in the energy."
2. One clip only, played slowly. Pauses, slow motion and replays on the key beat.
3. A calm voice narrates what each dog is doing, like a coach breaking down game film.
4. Short on-screen labels on the action: "pressure", "teeth", "barking", "calm, confident".
5. The payoff is one idea the owner can use: "calm, confident energy."
6. The caption asks a question: "Are these videos helpful?" That drives comments.
7. He uses other people's footage under fair use. We do not.

## Our version
- **Footage:** only our own clips from the library. Play groups, intros, the first meeting at drop off, a shy dog warming up, a dog choosing rest.
- **Voice:** Alex or Amanda, recorded on the phone, reading in a calm coach's voice. Their real voices build trust and set up Board and Train authority. A Higgsfield voice is only for testing.
- **Length:** 30 to 60 seconds. One clip, one lesson.
- **Edit:** slow motion and a freeze on the key beat, a circle or arrow on the dog, 1 to 3 word labels in our brand type, and burned-in captions.
- **Payoff:** one thing the owner can try at home, or one thing we watch for when a new dog arrives.
- **Caption:** end on a question ("Want more of these?"). Then the normal call to action.

## Rules
- No health or safety claims. Never say a dog is "safe", and never diagnose.
- Describe what we see, not labels. "He turns his head away" beats "he is fearful aggressive".
- No breed generalizations (business-facts.md). Every dog is its own dog.
- Never show or describe a fight. Show calm handling, space and good choices.
- Our own footage only. No reposts.

## First 5 ideas (Codex picks and orders)
1. "Watch his tail and ears": a new dog meets the group, slowed down.
2. "This is what calm looks like": a team member stands still and the dog settles.
3. "Why we let him walk away": a dog chooses space, and we respect it.
4. "Play or too much?": a play bow, a pause, then back to play.
5. "The look that means 'give me a minute'": a shy dog at drop off.
