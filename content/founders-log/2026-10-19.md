## Substack post

**Title:** The ad my AI wrote that I would never say

**Subtitle:** Founder's Log 02. Nine new ads, one sentence I had to kill, and what "premium" actually means at a dog boarding home.

It was Saturday morning and I was reading my own ads like a stranger would. [Alex: adjust the scene. Where were you, what time, coffee or no coffee?]

Nine of them had gone live the night before. New images, new words, the biggest creative refresh we have done all year. I had approved them. I had said "launch."

And halfway down the third one I read a sentence I would never say to a customer.

"Every new dog starts with one paid overnight."

It was not a lie. A week earlier I had told my AI that was the rule. Then I changed my mind and never wrote it down anywhere it could see. So it did exactly what I had told it to do, in nine ads, to strangers.

Then I found the second one. "Pick up before 10 AM and your last day is free."

That one is true too. It is how our billing works. But sitting inside an ad, it read like a coupon. And we do not do coupons.

---

Here is the backstory.

For six months one single static ad has carried our paid leads. One image with our reviews on it. Over that time it brought in 361 leads at about $17 each. That is a good ad. It is also a tired ad, and the only one we had.

So this week I tried something I saw Alex Hormozi doing. He tests dozens of image ads at once. Big bold statements, hand drawn sketches, real reviews, notes app screenshots. I had Claude build 23 of them. We picked the best nine, and I put them in the same campaign with the same budget. No new spend.

I also learned that an old image I had paused back in May brought leads at $9 each. Half the cost of anything running now. Nobody had moved it over when we changed campaigns. It just sat there.

---

The part I keep thinking about is why that sentence bothered me so much.

When people leave other boarding places and come to us, they tell us the same story. The price on the phone was $45 or $55 a night. Then the bill showed up. Extra for 30 more minutes of play. Extra for a toy. A bath they had to pay for at drop off and again at pick up. They paid once and then they paid again.

We built Four Paws Inn to be the opposite of that. One price. Nothing added at pick up. You pull up, you text us, and your dog comes to your car. Most families never get out of their seat.

A premium brand is not the one with the nicest photos. It is the one with no surprises.

So a discount, even a small one, even a true one, says the wrong thing about who we are. I paused all nine ads that morning, and we rewrote them before they came back on.

---

So here is where I am.

The nine ads are back on. They now test three different reasons to choose us: our reviews, how we care for each dog, and the honest line, "We are not the cheapest boarding in Miramar. We are the place families come back to." On Saturday I will know which reason people actually respond to. I genuinely do not know which one will win.

What I am taking from this week:

When you change a rule, write it down where everyone who works for you can see it. That includes the software. My AI was not wrong. My instructions were out of date.

And it is easy to say "premium." It is harder to cut the things that quietly undercut it.

Alex
