## Primary text A (the kid analogy, AD60 to AD63)
Would you leave your kid with strangers for a week, in a house they've never seen? No. So don't do it to your dog.

Holiday spots at Four Paws Inn go to dogs who have stayed with us before. New dogs start with one night.

One night is all it takes. They meet us, sleep here, and get picked up. They learn you always come back. The next time, they walk in like it's their second home. That's what our regulars do.

One night starts at $60. Your dog is worth it.

Send us a date for their first night. Holiday spots are going fast.

## Primary text B (call our bluff, AD64 and AD65)
Call our bluff. Give us one night.

One on one time first. A slow intro to the group when they're ready. A video update so you can watch them settle in.

Going away for the holidays? This is the night that matters most. Holiday spots go to dogs who already know us.

From $60. Send us a date.

Headline: "Your dog's first night, before the holidays." Button: Get quote.

## What was kept out, and why
- "We will transform your pup in a night" / "We will deliver": an outcome promise about a dog's behavior is a guarantee we can't control (CLAUDE.md gate 5) and invites Meta review trouble. "Call our bluff, then decide" carries the same confidence without promising an outcome.
- "Feel safe": safety claims are off limits. Used "second home" and "know us" instead.
- "Be a bad dog parent" and profanity: the kid line makes them feel it without calling them names. Meta also limits ads that tell people what they are.
- $60 is the dog under 50 lb rate for one night with a pick up before 10 AM. Over 50 lb is $75, puppies $80, so the ads say "from $60".
