# Ask Amanda: shoot day 1 (Tue Oct 20, 2026)

11 videos, 3 questions each, 33 answers. One sitting, about 90 minutes.

**What you get out of it:**
- 11 YouTube videos, each 3 to 5 minutes.
- 33 Shorts and Reels: one a day for a month.
- Answers you can text to leads instead of typing them out.

**Where it posts:** the Four Paws Inn YouTube channel. Shorts also go to Instagram and Facebook once the noon reel slots open after Nov 21. Claude edits everything (cuts, captions, titles, b roll) and schedules it.

---

## Shoot plan

**Where:** the living room or the cat room, with a window in front of Amanda (soft daylight on her face, never behind her). Leave dogs or a dog bed in the background so viewers know where she is.

**When:** during the dogs' midday rest, so there's less barking.

**Camera:**
- Insta360 or iPhone, horizontal, at eye level, about 4 feet away.
- Frame her from the chest up, with space above her head.
- Claude crops the vertical Shorts from the horizontal video.

**Audio:** use a clip-on mic if you have one. Otherwise keep the camera close. Phones on silent, AC off if possible.

**Wardrobe:**
- A solid color: cream, white, soft pink or charcoal. No logos, no busy prints.
- Bring a second top. Change after video 6, so the Shorts don't all look like the same day.

**Props within reach:**
- A Ziploc bag of food (video 8).
- A phone showing a real daily update (video 6).
- A leash.
- One calm dog to sit with her for a few videos. Videos 1, 4 and 11 are best for this.

**How Alex runs it:**
1. Read each question off camera, then stay quiet.
2. Amanda restates the question in her first sentence: "Is it ok if it's my dog's first time away? Yes, and..." That lets every answer stand alone as a Short.
3. One take per answer. If she stumbles, she pauses, takes a breath, and starts that answer again. Claude cuts the mistakes.
4. After each video's 3 answers, she says the closing line once.

**Amanda reads the answers below the night before.** They're written in her voice, but she doesn't memorize them. She uses her own words: real is better than perfect.

**Rules for every answer:**
- Never say "safe".
- No health promises.
- Never say how many dogs we have.
- Never name another boarding place.

**Closing line for every video:** "If you're near Miramar and your pup needs a place to stay, send us your dates. We'd love to meet them."

**After the shoot:** drop all the files in Drive, in RAW, then "Ask Amanda Oct 20". Tell Claude. Claude delivers edits within 48 hours.

## Video 1: "First time boarding your dog? Watch this first"

- **Thumbnail:** "First time away?"
- **Cold open:** Amanda says "If it's your dog's first time away from home, I want you to hear this."

**Q1. "It's my dog's first time away from home. Will they be ok?"**
> If it's your dog's first time away from home, I totally get it. Almost every family who comes to us feels that way the first time. Here's what I tell them. Your dog doesn't have to be ready on day one. We don't rush anything. Each dog settles in at their own pace. Some walk in and make friends in an hour. Some need a day or two of quiet time with us first. Both are completely normal. And you're not left wondering. You get photos and videos every day, so you can see them playing, resting, and being themselves.

- **Short hook:** "Their first time away? Watch this."

**Q2. "How do you help a new dog settle in?"**
> How do we help a new dog settle in? Slowly, and on purpose. New dogs never go straight into the pack. First they get one on one time with us, so they learn our voices and know who we are. Then they get quiet time to sniff around and explore the house and the yard. Then, when they're ready, we bring them into the group a little at a time. We watch their body language the whole way. If they need more time, they get more time.

- **Short hook:** "What really happens on day one."

**Q3. "Should we do a short stay before a long trip?"**
> Should you do a short stay before a long trip? For a lot of pups, yes, and here's why. One overnight lets us build a bond with your dog before the big trip. They sleep here, they meet us, they see the yard. So when they come back for a week, it's not new anymore. It already feels familiar. It's not required. It's something we suggest, especially for shy pups or first timers.

- **Short hook:** "The one thing to do before a long trip."

## Video 2: "My dog doesn't like other dogs. Can they still board?"

- **Thumbnail:** "Doesn't like dogs?"
- **Cold open:** "This is the question I get the most, and the answer might surprise you."

**Q4. "My dog doesn't like other dogs. Can they still stay?"**
> My dog doesn't like other dogs, can they still stay? The first thing I ask is: why do you feel that way? Because "doesn't like dogs" can mean a lot of things. Sometimes it means shy. Sometimes it means they had one bad experience at a dog park. Sometimes they just never got the chance to learn. A lot of pups whose parents told us this ended up playing with the pack after a few hours, or a few days. We never force it, and we always have options.

- **Short hook:** "Your dog might not be what you think."

**Q5. "What happens if my dog is shy?"**
> What happens if your dog is shy? Honestly, shy dogs are some of my favorites. Shy just means they need more time. We go slow. We let them watch the other dogs from a distance first. We read their energy every day. Are they curious? Are they relaxed? Are they ready for one new friend? We decide day by day, and they lead.

- **Short hook:** "Shy dogs need this one thing."

**Q6. "Do you take every dog?"**
> Do we take every dog? No, and I think that's important to say. If a dog is showing real signs of aggression with other dogs, we don't put them in the group. We have to protect every family's pet, not just one. But a dog who's a little nervous, or who might snap if they feel cornered, is different. That's a dog we can work with. That's why we always ask a lot of questions first. It helps us do right by your dog and everyone else's.

- **Short hook:** "Why we say no to some dogs."

## Video 3: "How we introduce dogs to the pack"

- **Thumbnail:** "How dogs meet the pack"
- **Cold open:** "This is exactly how we introduce a new dog to the pack."

**Q7. "Why do you keep your groups small?"**
> Why do we keep our groups small? Because it's better for the dogs. Small groups mean shy and picky dogs can actually relax. And it means we get to know every single dog one on one. We know who likes to wrestle, who likes to lie in the sun, and who wants to be next to us all day. That's hard to do with a big crowd.

- **Short hook:** "Why small groups matter."

**Q8. "How do you know when a dog is ready for the group?"**
> How do we know when a dog is ready for the group? We watch their body. A ready dog has a loose, wiggly body. Soft eyes. Curious sniffing. Maybe a play bow, where the front legs go down and the tail goes up. A dog who isn't ready yet looks stiff, hides, or keeps their tail tucked. We don't guess. We wait for the dog to tell us.

- **Short hook:** "Signs your dog is ready to make friends."

**Q9. "Do you take puppies and senior dogs?"**
> Do we take puppies and seniors? Yes, we love both. Puppies get lots of short play and lots of naps, because they get tired fast. Seniors get a slower day. More rest, cozy beds, and gentle friends. Every dog's day is built around who they are, not just their age.

- **Short hook:** "Puppies vs seniors: how their days differ."

## Video 4: "Two dogs? Here's how siblings stay with us"

- **Thumbnail:** "Siblings stay together"
- **Cold open:** "If you have two dogs, this one rule matters most."

**Q10. "I have two dogs. Will they stay together?"**
> Will your two dogs stay together? Always. Siblings stay together here, no matter what. They came from the same home, they sleep together at home, and being together helps them feel calm here too. That's a rule we never break.

- **Short hook:** "Our number one rule for two dog families."

**Q11. "What if one of my dogs is social and the other is shy?"**
> What if one dog is social and the other is shy? That happens a lot. The social one can go out and play with the pack. When they're done playing, they come right back inside to their brother or sister. And the shy one can go out in the yard with their sibling, so they're never alone. Each one gets what they need, and they still end the day together.

- **Short hook:** "One shy dog, one party dog."

**Q12. "Do you have private rooms?"**
> Do we have private rooms? Yes. Some dogs do best with their own space, especially on a first stay. A lot of times we start a pup in a private room for a day or two, then slowly bring them into the pack when they're ready. It's not forever. It's a starting point, and we adjust day by day.

- **Short hook:** "Not every dog wants the pack, and that's ok."
