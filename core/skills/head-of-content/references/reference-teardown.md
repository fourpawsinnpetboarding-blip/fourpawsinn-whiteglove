# Reference teardown

Use this on every post you study before borrowing its idea or format. Ideas are cheap; the detail is what makes them work. Read the post with `eden_read_social_post` (transcript and on screen text) and write these six lines.

1. **First frame element count.** Every text box, badge, logo, face, and subject counts. Over 6 is a warning sign: the viewer cannot tell where to look.
2. **Proof element.** What on screen makes the claim believable: a real number, a dashboard, a review count, a before and after. None is a gap we can fill.
3. **First line.** Quote it. Does it talk to the viewer ("you", "your") or about the creator?
4. **Promise.** What payoff does the first 3 seconds set up? ("here are 5 things", "watch what happens")
5. **Pace.** Rough seconds per shot or per on screen line.
6. **Copy and change.** What we keep, and what we change for our own audience and proof.

## Rules for our own drafts
- Visual hook: 6 elements or fewer, one headline, one proof element, subject fills the frame.
- Verbal hook: speak to the viewer and promise a payoff.
- The client's proof element and examples live in the client facts file and the client's hook rules (for Four Paws Inn: `content/video/hook-rules.md`).

Source of the method: Devin Jatho's visual hook and verbal hook teardown (instagram.com/p/Dc24oGkA-Ip).
