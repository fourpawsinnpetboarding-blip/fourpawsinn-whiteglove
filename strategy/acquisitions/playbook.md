# Acquisition playbook: buy a pet boarding location by December 2027

Owner: Alex. Research and prep: Claude. Updated after every call.

## The one rule

Our constraint is demand, not capacity. Buying empty kennels does not help us. **We buy customers, not buildings.** The target is a business whose existing client list, reviews and bookings come with it, and that our marketing machine can fill further.

## Vocabulary (use these words on every call)

- **SDE** (seller's discretionary earnings): profit plus everything the owner pays themselves or runs through the business. The number small businesses are priced on.
- **Multiple:** asking price divided by SDE. Small service businesses often trade around 2x to 3x SDE.
- **Add backs:** personal or one time expenses the seller adds back to boost SDE. Always ask for the list and proof.
- **CIM** (confidential information memorandum): the broker's sales packet. You get it after signing an NDA.
- **NDA** (non disclosure agreement): you promise to keep their numbers private. Normal, sign it.
- **LOI:** your written offer. Price, terms, due diligence period, contingencies.
- **Due diligence:** the 30 to 90 days you verify everything before closing.
- **Seller financing** (seller note): the seller lets you pay part of the price over time. Shows they believe in the business.
- **SBA 7(a):** the government backed loan most small business buyers use. Expect to put roughly 10% down and show the business cash flow covers the payment with room to spare.
- **DSCR** (debt service coverage ratio): cash flow divided by yearly loan payments. Lenders want it comfortably above 1.
- **Owner dependence:** how much the business stops working if the owner leaves. The biggest hidden risk in small deals.
- **Working capital:** cash needed to run day to day after you take over.

## Call scripts

### Broker call (listed deals)

> Hi, this is Alex Chiong. I own Four Paws Inn, a home based dog boarding business in Miramar. We do about $30,000 a month with 215 Google reviews. I'm looking to buy a second location in Broward or north Miami Dade in the next 12 months. I saw your listing for [business]. Is it still available?
>
> Great. Before I sign the NDA, can you tell me three things: roughly what share of revenue is boarding, whether the real estate is leased or owned, and why the owner is selling?

Then: sign the NDA, get the CIM, send it to Claude.

### Owner call (off market, businesses not for sale)

> Hi, is this the owner? My name is Alex. I own Four Paws Inn in Miramar, we board dogs too. This isn't a sales call. I'm looking to grow by partnering with or buying a respected boarding business nearby, and yours came up. Have you ever thought about what you'd do with the business someday?

If no: "Totally understand. Would it be okay if I checked back in six months?" Log it. Many deals come from the second or third call.

If maybe: move to discovery questions. Never talk price on the first call.

### How to sound experienced

1. Ask, then stop talking. Let them fill the silence.
2. Never say a number first.
3. Say "help me understand" instead of "why".
4. Ask for documents, not stories: "Can you show me that on the tax returns?"
5. End every call with a next step and a date.

## Discovery questions

**Business**
1. What share of revenue is boarding, daycare, grooming, training?
2. How many dogs can you board, and how full are you on an average night? On holidays?
3. What are your nightly or daily rates? When did you last raise them?
4. How many active clients? How many book again each year?
5. Where do new clients come from?

**Owner and people**
6. How many hours a week do you work in the business? What would break if you disappeared for a month?
7. Who are your key employees, and how long have they been there?
8. Why are you selling? Why now?

**Money**
9. What was SDE for the last three years? Can I see the tax returns?
10. What add backs are in that number?
11. Would you consider carrying part of the price as a seller note?

**Property and compliance**
12. Lease or owned? Years left? Can it be assigned to a buyer?
13. Any code, zoning, noise or neighbor issues? Any open violations?
14. Any bite incidents, injuries or claims in the last five years? What insurance do you carry?

## Deal math: no money down or no money left

Source prompt: "No Money Down? Or No Money Left? The Math & Logic of Deal Making" (YouTube, O8w3zKqGB_M). The rule below is ours, run on our numbers.

**The rule:** a deal is only good if the business can pay the loan, pay a manager to replace the owner, and still survive a bad year. "No money down" does not make a deal cheap. It moves the whole price onto monthly payments.

**Always subtract a manager first.** SDE assumes the owner works for free. We will not run a second location ourselves, so take out a manager's pay (plan on $50,000 a year) before you count anything.

### Worked example: Miami lead ($198,000 asking, $150,000 listed SDE)

| | SBA 7(a), 10% down | 100% seller note, no money down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash at closing | about $20,000 | $0 |
| Loan | $178,000, 10 years, about 10.5% | $198,000, 5 years, 7% |
| Yearly payments | about $29,000 | about $47,000 |
| SDE minus $50,000 manager | $100,000 | $100,000 |
| Left after payments | about $71,000 | about $53,000 |
| DSCR (cash flow divided by loan payments) | about 3.4 | about 2.1 |
| **Bad case:** real SDE is $100,000, not $150,000 | $21,000 left | **$3,000 left** |

The bad case is common. It happens when the add backs don't survive due diligence. At $3,000 a year, one slow summer or one repair puts us underwater. That is the "no money left" deal.

### How we structure offers

1. Base: SBA 7(a) with about 10% down. Longer term means lower payments.
2. Ask for a seller note of 10 to 20% of the price, paid after the SBA loan. It keeps the seller invested in the transition, and lenders like it.
3. Run the bad case every time: cut SDE by a third, subtract the manager, subtract the payments. If less than $25,000 a year is left, the price is too high.
4. Keep 3 months of operating costs in cash after closing (working capital). Never spend the reserve on the down payment.
5. Walk away from any deal that only works at 100% financing.

## Red flags

- Numbers on the listing do not match the tax returns.
- Owner does everything, no staff who can run a day alone.
- Lease has less than 3 years left, or the landlord will not transfer it.
- Zoning or city permits unclear. (Our own code compliance notice in September 2026 is the lesson.)
- Revenue falling for two years with no clear reason.
- Seller refuses any seller note or transition period.
- Reviews mention safety incidents.
