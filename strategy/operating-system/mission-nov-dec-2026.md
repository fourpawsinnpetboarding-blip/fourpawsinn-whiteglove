# The mission (Alex, Nov 4, 2026)

$40,000 in November. $50,000 in December. One mission.

The number one job: dial in content (static, reels, organic, long form, ad creatives) so any new offer plugs into a system that already works. Then we know how many creatives we need, the budget, the leads, and the revenue.

Out of scope until this works: influencer outreach and trainer hiring (daily routine stopped Nov 4), Board and Train rollout, any research not about content.

## The chain we are building
Content that works -> we know why -> creatives per offer -> budget -> leads -> booked revenue.
Revenue = leads x sales conversion rate x average stay. Long form content raises the conversion rate (people trust it).

## Content diagnosis v1 (Nov 4, from Eden: our last 60 days + market breakouts)

### Our numbers
- 314 posts in 60 days (about 10 a week). Peers post about 0.6 a week.
- Engagement rate 0.35% vs peer cohort 3.06%: we are about 9x below peers. Our posts sit at the 11th percentile.
- Most posts reach 200 to 270 people, which is roughly our follower base. Only a few broke out.

### What worked (our own outliers)
1. Real footage reels with a clear parent question or process, early October: "Before you compare boarding prices" 938 views, 11,785 watch seconds, 13 shares; "What your dog sees on day one" 869; "What drop off looks like" 776; "How we customize your dog's stay" 703. About 5.5% engagement and 600 reach, 3x our normal.
2. Review statics with a real story: Tamara's senior dog (4.9x baseline), Amy's shy girl (3.5x), "drop off feels like the first day of school" (3.8x).

### What failed
1. Music montages with a generic caption ("little moments", "backyard break", "time with people"): 230 to 260 views, under 1% engagement.
2. Generic dog fact carousels: about 2 likes each.
3. Pure price reels: below baseline.
4. Volume: 10 posts a week split attention; peers win with fewer, better posts.

### What breaks out in the market (Eden, last quarter, 3x+ outliers)
1. A real person on camera showing the unglamorous truth of the work. Top hit: a boarding and training owner, "Come break dogs with me in 100 degree weather", 284 seconds long, 8M views, 106x his baseline.
2. Day in the life vlogs with a named dog and a human voice, 1 to 5 minutes.
3. Small business owners on camera explaining their own business.

## Answers to Alex's questions (v1, to be tested)
| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Better editing? | Cleaner, not fancier. Hook in the first 2 seconds, captions, real sound. | Our montages with music lost; our winners were simple real footage with a clear line. |
| More authentic videos? | Yes. This is the biggest lever. | Every winner (ours and the market's) is real people and real dogs. |
| Longer content? | Yes, for trust: 60 to 120 second reels, plus long form on YouTube. | The market's top boarding post runs 284 seconds. Long form raises conversion. |
| Shorter? | Keep short only for ad hooks and Trial Reels. | Short tests the hook cheaply; long builds the trust that books. |
| Better static? | Fewer, story driven review statics. Drop generic facts. | Reviews with a real story beat facts by 3x to 5x. |
| Who on camera? | Amanda and Alex, talking to the phone. | People trust a face. The faceless pages are the weakest. |
