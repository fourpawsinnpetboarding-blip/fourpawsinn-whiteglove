**Money definitions (use these exact words in every report):**
- **Lead** = form submitted (Meta count).
- **Qualified lead / qualified conversation** = lead contacted and confirmed as a fit for dates/services (GHL stage, human judgment).
- **Booking / booked value** = stay reserved with dates and rate (value at booking, before the stay occurs).
- **Deposit** = cash collected at booking — part of booked value, NOT additional to it.
- **Collected cash / completed-stay revenue** = cash actually received for a stay that happened. Deposits for future stays are not completed-stay revenue. Cancellations and no-shows subtract from booked value.
- Counting a deposit AND the full booked value AND the final payment as three revenues for one stay double- or triple-counts. Never do it.

**Cohort judging (no same-day verdicts):** Daily/weekly views are for execution problems only (spend, clicks, tracking breaks). Judge quality on a 30-day cohort (lead → qualified → meet & greet rate), pipeline on a 60-day cohort (booked value), and cash on a 90-day cohort (completed stays, collected cash). A campaign launched last week cannot be judged by the same revenue standard as one running for months — its leads have not had time to become stays.

### 2.6 The November $40,000 goal and the "18 leads a day" test

**November status [Verified Nov 4]:** The "November Daily Revenue" tab's TOTAL row reads **$15,600** in logged November stay value. (Caution applied: the tab also contains a "Monthly Total $15,600" row — the $31,200 figure you get by summing every subtotal row double-counts that monthly row. $15,600 is the tab's own total, and it reconciles to the Bookings tab's November stays.) **Gap to $40,000: $24,400.**

Supporting figures: Bookings tab shows 52 stays overlapping November, average TOTAL REV $359 per stay **[My calculation]**; November-portion average ≈ $340 per stay ($15,600 across ~46 daily-tab blocks). Deposits recorded against November-overlapping stays total $7,196 — but those are full-stay deposits, not November cash collected, so do not use them as "November revenue so far." Some November stays may not yet be logged (calendar-only holds exist), so $15,600 is "logged," not "final."

**Testing 18 leads/day [My calculations, assumptions stated]:**
- 18 × 30 = 540 leads in a month.
- At Meta's measured 5.3% lead→booked rate: 540 × 5.3% ≈ 29 bookings × ~$350 ≈ **$10,150** — about 40% of the $24,400 gap, 25% of the $40,000 goal.
- At the all-source 34.1% rate: 540 × 34.1% ≈ 184 bookings × $350 ≈ $64,000 — which only shows that the rate assumption is everything, and the all-source rate is not an ad rate.
- Gap math: $24,400 ÷ $350 ≈ **70 more November bookings needed**. At 5.3% that needs ~1,320 leads; at 15% (if qualification improves) ~467 leads; at 30% ~233 leads.
- Budget math: 540 leads × ~$20 CPL ≈ $10,800/month, vs. ~$2,000/month current Meta spend and ~2.7 Meta leads/day in October. 18/day is roughly 7x current lead volume and 5x current spend at current CPL.
- Timing math: the goal counts stays *happening* in November. Leads generated now can still fill Thanksgiving week (Nov 22–29, the densest block on the books); leads for December trips do not count at all.

**Verdict: 18 leads/day is not supported as a sufficient plan at Meta's observed conversion.** It is the right order of magnitude ONLY if the qualification→booking rate improves dramatically. The better November target is not a lead count — it is qualified conversations per week and November-dated bookings per week, with the lead count as an input, not the goal. (Proposing that reframe is a recommendation, not a measured fact.)

**The unit-economics sting [My calculation]:** Cost per Meta booking = CPL ÷ conversion rate. At 5.3%: $16.83 CPL → ~$317 per booking; $22.18 → ~$418; $25 → ~$472. Against a ~$350 average stay value, Meta at current conversion is roughly break-even on booked value *before* operating costs. **The lever is conversion rate, not cheaper leads.** Cutting CPL from $22 to $18 while conversion stays 5.3% saves ~$75 per booking; lifting conversion from 5.3% to 10% at $22 CPL cuts cost per booking from ~$418 to ~$220. That is why the qualification sprint (§1) outranks any budget or creative move this month.

## 3. Campaign structure: add, separate, or new campaign?

**Where things live [Meta guidance]:** Campaign = objective (locked at creation). Ad set = audience, placements, schedule, budget (when campaign budget is off), optimization event, bid strategy. Ad = creative only. Budget lives at exactly one level: campaign level if Advantage campaign budget is on, ad-set level if off.

**Decision rules:**
- **Add an ad to an existing ad set** when audience, objective, optimization event, and budget are identical AND you accept the learning cost (adding an ad is a significant edit that restarts learning). Never add challengers to a winning ad set you intend to protect.
- **Create a separate ad set** when the audience, optimization event, placement set, or budget guarantee differs, or when a test needs protected spend that campaign-budget allocation would starve. Ad-set budgets are the tool for "this test must get its spend."
- **Start a new campaign** when the objective differs, the budget strategy differs, or you need a clean test unit. A new campaign does NOT isolate audiences by itself — two campaigns targeting the same people still overlap in auctions, and Meta enters only its highest-total-value ad in each auction, so losers get no delivery.
- **One campaign/ad set per creative at small budgets is the worst structure.** Each ad set must independently reach ~50 optimization events in the week after its last significant edit to exit learning **[Meta guidance]**; below that it is Learning Limited — Meta says this "isn't a penalty" but indicates budget "isn't being spent effectively," and lists "running too many ads at the same time" among its causes. Fragmentation simultaneously starves each cell, creates overlap, and leaves most creatives at near-zero spend — which is exactly what the nine Oct 16 images and AD73 show.

**Campaign budget vs. ad-set budget [Meta guidance + Practitioner opinion]:** With Advantage campaign budget, Meta distributes one campaign budget continuously toward the best opportunities — deliberately unequal. Judge results at the campaign level. Practitioners converge: ad-set budgets when a test needs a guaranteed allocation; campaign budget when ad sets are proven and you want Meta to shift spend to winners. At tens of dollars/day, campaign budget across several ad sets will concentrate spend on one early leader and starve challengers — efficient for scaling proven winners, hostile to fair challenger testing. **Test under ad-set budgets (or Experiments); graduate winners into campaign-budget scaling.**

**Campaign names lie.** A name is free text. The only trustworthy check is Ads Manager: the campaign's Budget column and Budget strategy setting, plus each ad set's budget field. Two traps this account already demonstrates: a "$10/day" campaign running at $40/day, and a "$1400 test" running on $350 lifetime.

## 4. Learning, fresh creative, and true A/B tests

**What restarts learning [Meta guidance — significant-edit list]:** Always significant: any targeting change; any ad creative change; any optimization-event change; adding a new ad to the ad set; pausing the ad set for 7+ days; changing bid strategy. Significant depending on magnitude: budget amount; bid/cost controls; ad-set spending limit (Meta's example: $100 → $101 unlikely to restart; $100 → $1,000 may). Not on the list: renaming, changing an end date, switching one ad off — though Meta notes every edit "has some effect on delivery." During learning, Meta says ad sets "are less stable and usually have a higher CPA." A restart is not a deletion of history, but delivery stability and cost predictability reset.

**Does adding creatives necessarily disrupt a winner?** No — necessarily is too strong, and this account is the counterexample to the simple story: the nine images did not steal spend and delivery metrics did not collapse (§2.4). But adding creatives DOES restart learning by Meta's definition, and Meta's own advice is to test new ads in a separate ad set. So: adding creatives to a winner is a real risk with a real mechanism, not a proven cause of this decline. **Practical rule: batch edits together so they trigger one reset instead of several, and avoid daily tinkering.**

**How much evidence does a test need? [Meta guidance + Practitioner opinion, mutually inconsistent — which is itself informative]:**
- Meta's bar: ~50 optimization events per ad set per week to exit learning; A/B tests need at least 7 days (some pages say 14), results shown after 100 events but judged at test end. Budget arithmetic: daily budget ≈ 50 × cost per result ÷ 7. At $20/lead that is ~$143/day per ad set — far above this account's budgets. **Expect to live in Learning Limited permanently, and judge tests by pre-committed spend/time rules, not by waiting for learning exit or significance that will never arrive.**
- Practitioner sample guidance ranges from "30–50 conversions per variation before reading anything" to "100 conversions per variation at 95% confidence when the CPA gap exceeds 30%; 400+ when under 15%"; CTR-only reads need ~1,000 clicks per variation. A practical shortcut offered: a clear outperformer after 3 days and 30 conversions can be scaled without formal significance; a clear underperformer after 5 days can be cut. Pause heuristics from one audit framework: never pause the top-converting ad in an ad set; pause at 0 conversions with spend > 2x the ad-set CPA target; pause at CPA > 3x the ad-set average only with ≥ 3 conversions; 1–2 conversions at high CPA = watch; an ad under 7 days old = watch. **[Practitioner opinion — adopt as heuristics, not rules.]**
- Applied here: at $40/day and ~$20/lead, one ad set produces ~14 leads/week. Split five ways, each hook gets ~3 leads/week — one lead swings "CPL" wildly and proves nothing. Hence batching (§1) and judging on delivery + CTR + qualified conversations first, CPL second.

**True A/B test vs. ordinary comparison [Meta guidance]:**
- **Experiments A/B test:** audience randomly split into non-overlapping groups; one variable changes; equal budgets per version recommended; up to 5 versions; minimum 7 days, maximum 30. Winner = lowest cost per result, with a reported confidence percentage. Confidence thresholds below which Meta calls results "similar": 65% for 2 variants, 40% for 3, 35% for 4, 30% for 5. Meta does not recommend testing by turning ad sets on/off (overlapping audiences).
- **Ordinary comparison (everything this account has done):** multiple ads in one ad set, or parallel ad sets, with no split. The spend leader is "which ad Meta's algorithm preferred early" — not "which ad performs better" **[Practitioner opinion]**. Same users can see both variants; delivery is biased by pacing and early signals. Useful for *screening*, not for winner declarations.
- **Middle option — Creative Test:** compares 2–7 new variants inside an existing campaign using part of its budget (Meta suggests ≤ 20%), over a chosen duration and metric. Produces a relative ranking *without* a confidence level. Not equivalent to an Experiments test **[Practitioner opinion, citing Meta Help Center and Jon Loomer]**.
- **Promoting a successful test without assuming carry-over:** a winner in a test ad set does not automatically perform in the scaling campaign — different budget, pacing, audience composition, and competition. Graduate by duplicating the winner into the scaling structure and re-measuring on qualified conversations and bookings over a full cohort window; do not assume the test CPL transfers.

## 5. Stagnant campaigns: diagnosis before action

**Distinguish the eight causes by checking in this order** (each step isolates a different cause; a drop in submissions alone identifies none of them):
1. **Budget/audience/placement changes** — check the activity log first. Found in this account: budget changes, no audience change found.
2. **Delivery** — impressions/reach collapsed? Audience/budget/rejection, not creative. Here: held.
3. **Creative/audience fit** — CTR/link clicks fell while impressions held? Here: held.
4. **Form friction** — opens held but submissions fell after a form change? Needs form history + opens vs. submissions. Instant Forms come in More Volume (pre-filled, no review step) and Higher Intent (adds a review screen) **[Meta guidance]**; practitioners report Higher Intent cuts raw volume 20–40% but lifts contact/qualified rates materially — ranges, not benchmarks. Use the account's own before/after on the same form, never a universal benchmark (published "healthy" completion rates conflict: below 60% vs. above 30%).
5. **CRM delivery** — Meta lead count vs. GHL count diverged? Sync failure (Leads Access, expired login, wrong form watched, CRM field rejection), not lead quality. Diagnostic order: Leads Center first, then a test lead via Meta's Lead Ads Testing Tool **[Practitioner opinion]**.
6. **Lead quality** — submissions held but qualified/conversation rate fell? Offer/audience/intent, not form mechanics.
7. **Tracking** — attribution window (7-day click / 1-day view here), missing click IDs, duplicate events (dedup needs matching eventID + event name within 48 hours **[Meta guidance]**), or a later CRM outcome reusing an earlier event's ID (don't — each stage change is its own event **[Practitioner opinion]**).
8. **Seasonality** — future-trip planners click now and book later; a November-stay business will always show a lag between lead week and stay week.

**Before refresh / rebuild / pause / restart, decide by the check:**
- Refresh (new creative, same ad set) only if steps 2–3 point at creative AND you accept a learning restart.
- Rebuild (new ad set) if audience, optimization event, or form changed — history does not transfer, so only rebuild when the old structure is the problem.
- Pause only if the campaign is actively losing money on a mature cohort AND you accept that 7+ days paused restarts learning on resume.
- Restart (resume after pause, or relaunch) is not a reset button: resumed ad sets re-enter learning; relaunched ones start with no history. Prefer resuming a paused winner as-is over rebuilding it.

## 6. Instagram Trial Reels: the free testing channel

**Availability and eligibility [Meta guidance]:** A trial reel is an ordinary reel posted with a Trial switch on before sharing. Instagram shows it to non-followers first. Launched Dec 10, 2024. Current Help Center conditions: a **public** account; at least **200 followers** for a professional (business/creator) account or **1,000** for a personal account; and eligibility to appear in recommendations (check Account Status if the toggle disappears — losing recommendation eligibility removes trial access until restored). **The toggle in the app is the ground truth** — if it appears when posting, the account qualifies. Four Paws Inn's own eligibility was not verified in-app in this research; check it on @fourpawsinnpetboarding directly. Trial reels can be scheduled in-app and posted via API; there is no trial carousel, photo, or story.

**How distribution works [Meta guidance]:** Trial viewers are algorithm-selected strangers via the recommendations system. Followers do NOT see it in Feed or the Reels tab and it does NOT appear on the profile grid until shared to everyone. Only the poster can see that a reel is a trial (it sits next to drafts, visible only to them). It is not perfectly sealed: followers may still encounter it via DM shares, same-audio pages, or search. "Trial reels are evaluated independently in Instagram's ranking and don't impact the performance or ranking of your standard reels content." Trials may take longer to get views because the audience is strangers. **How Instagram picks trial viewers is unverified** — no Meta page found explains it. Do not assume trial viewers are in Miramar/Pembroke Pines or are pet owners.

**Graduating [Meta guidance]:** Manual any time (open reel → View insights → Share to everyone), or automatic if "Share to everyone automatically" was turned on before sharing (it stays on for future trials until changed; the current Help Center names no window or threshold — only "performing well soon after you publish it, based on signals like views and engagement"). **For testing, turn auto-share OFF** — if one variant graduates mid-test while another is trial-only, the comparison breaks **[Practitioner opinion]**.

**Metrics that matter [Meta guidance via Mosseri, Jan–Feb 2025, + Practitioner opinion]:** Compare trials to trials, never to normal reels. Top ranking signals: watch time, likes, sends — with sends slightly more important for unconnected content. Practitioner priority order: (1) sends/shares per reach — strongest stranger-value signal, closest organic proxy to "would someone act"; (2) average watch time and retention, including first-3-seconds skip rate; (3) saves per reach; (4) likes per reach; (5) profile visits and follows; (6) views — LAST (a view counts after a very short watch; views measure delivery, not interest). Trial insights appear within ~24 hours; practitioner consensus is a first look at 24 hours and the main decision at **72 hours**. Meta publishes no minimum view count for a valid decision. The defensible rule is relative: judge each trial against the **median of your own previous trials** on sends-per-reach and average watch time — not against a universal number and not against your best trial **[Hypothesis]**. A trial with 200 views and strong sends-per-reach beats one with 2,000 views and near-zero sends. Near-zero views usually means a distribution/eligibility/content problem (check Account Status; check for reused content), not a creative verdict.

**Limitations — why organic views do NOT prove paid-lead potential:**
1. Different audience: unknown location, age, pet ownership. A reel can "win" with viewers in other states who will never board a dog in Miramar.
2. Different intent: entertainment mode vs. a booking offer with a form.
3. Different friction: follow/DM vs. form fill, call, or deposit weeks later — especially for future-trip planners.
4. No conversion signal exists in trial insights: no leads, calls, bookings, revenue, or lead quality.
5. No offer or price test: a hook can win on humor and fail the moment the viewer sees rates and dates.
6. Follower gap: trials deliberately exclude followers, so they cannot tell you how past clients and warm prospects — the best lead source — would react.
**Bottom line:** a trial win means "this opening holds strangers' attention and earns shares" — necessary, not sufficient, for a paid lead winner. The paid test is still required, judged on qualified inquiries and booked value.

**Gotchas:** A trial reel cannot be boosted or run as an ad while in trial mode — share it to everyone first, then select it as an existing post in Ads Manager **[Practitioner opinion]**. Re-posting the same content organically hurts reach ("may get limited reach if Instagram detects that you've previously shared the same content" **[Meta guidance]**) — so share the trial itself rather than re-uploading it. Music: business accounts must use the Meta Sound Collection or licensed/voiceover audio — a personal-use trending track can be muted, blocked, or claimed, and a reel with one cannot be promoted as an ad **[Meta guidance via practitioner reporting, checked Aug–Oct 2026]**. Keep trials under 3 minutes (reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to new audiences, and a trial's entire audience is new — provisional, verify in-app). No verified Facebook trial-reels feature exists — do not plan around it.

## 7. Sequencing and value-first advertising: what is actually possible

**Native ad sequencing [Practitioner opinion — rollout, not every account has it]:** Meta has rolled out ad sequencing for auction campaigns (previously locked behind Reservation campaigns). Reported requirements: Awareness objective with "Maximize reach of ads" OR Engagement with conversion location "On Your Ad" and "Maximize ThruPlay views"; NO Sales, Traffic, or Leads objective; Target frequency control (not default Cap); lifetime budget only; minimum 7 days; Advantage Campaign Budget off; at least 2 ads; the toggle appears only AFTER publishing the ad set. Reported guidance: 2–3 ads optimal (up to 50 possible, with major drop-off); each ad must work standalone since not everyone sees the full sequence. Bugs with Target-frequency publishing were reported Feb 2026; current status unverified, and Four Paws Inn's account eligibility is unverified. **A Leads campaign cannot use native sequencing under the reported requirements.** Reservation (Reach and Frequency) buying can also sequence, but is Awareness/Engagement-only, requires a minimum audience size, and small-local eligibility is unverified.

**The realistic form for this business is an engagement ladder, not a guaranteed sequence [Hypothesis]:**
- Step 1: Helpful video (Amanda, no booking CTA or a soft one) to Miramar +15mi.
- Step 2: People who watched a meaningful share (50%+ or ThruPlay — NOT 3-second views, which practitioners call worthless as a signal) get the testimonial/booking ad.
- Step 3: People who engaged with step 2 (lead-form opens, 90-day retention; video engagement up to 365 days) get the offer/deposit ad.
- Exclusions are the load-bearing wall: exclude each later audience from the earlier steps, AND set exclusions as hard controls ("Further limit the reach of your ads," uncheck "Use as a suggestion") — under Advantage+ audience, included custom audiences are suggestions Meta may expand past, while location, minimum age, languages, and custom-audience exclusions are hard controls **[Meta guidance, as reported]**. Detailed-targeting exclusions were removed from Ads Manager Mar 31, 2025. Note: lookalike audiences cannot be excluded under Advantage+ audience.
- **What breaks strict order even with a ladder:** a prospecting/Advantage+ campaign delivering the step-2 creative to cold people; someone entering via organic views; audiences too small to deliver at step 2/3 (hard minimum 100 people, ~1,000 described as an effective minimum); a person booking by call/text with no Meta-trackable event, so the sequence looks "failed" in Ads Manager even if it worked; planning lag exceeding the retargeting recency window actually used.
- **There is no native "sequence completion" report.** What can be measured: audience size at each step over time, delivery/cost of the step-2 ad set to the step-1 audience, form submissions from the step-2 ad set, and — outside Meta — CRM cohorts by first lead date. None proves a person saw ads in order. For incrementality, a geographic holdout test answers what attribution cannot **[Practitioner opinion]**.

**Realistic local funnel for Four Paws Inn [Hypothesis]:**
1. Value-first video (helpful, local, Amanda) → broad Miramar +15mi, Leads or Awareness objective depending on whether native sequencing is available (it is not for Leads — so use the ladder).
2. 50%+ viewers (7–30 day window for "hot" retargeting, practitioner practice, not a Meta rule) → testimonial + first-sleepover offer ad.
3. Lead-form openers/submitters + page/IG engagers → deposit/holiday-availability ad, with past clients and already-booked people excluded.
4. Every step's creative must make sense to someone who skipped the previous step. Pool size is the binding constraint at each step, not creative order: if step 2's audience is under ~100–1,000 people or not delivering, the ladder stalls no matter how good the creative is.

## 8. Decision tree

```
New creative idea?
├─ Is it an opening/hook question? → Trial Reel first (5 days, §9). Trial median-beating on sends/reach + watch time AND carries a local, bookable offer?
│   ├─ YES → Paid test: batch of 2 in a NEW challenger ad set, ad-set budget. Judge per §10.
│   └─ NO → Drop it, or change ONE element and re-trial. Never paid-test a trial loser to "see."
├─ Is it a new offer/audience/form? → Separate ad set (or campaign if objective/budget strategy differs). Never add to the incumbent.
└─ Is the incumbent delivering profitably on a mature cohort? → YES: don't touch it. NO: diagnose per §5 before any change.

Challenger result after its pre-committed window?
├─ Delivery + CTR strong, qualified conversations arriving → Keep running; judge bookings on the 30-day cohort before scaling.
├─ Delivery strong, clicks cheap, no qualified conversations → Check form (opens vs submissions) and GHL delivery before blaming creative.
├─ No delivery (spend ~$0, few impressions) → Not a creative verdict. Check overlap, budget, audience size, rejections.
└─ Spend spent, CPL high, 1–2 conversions → Watch, don't pause (§10). High CPL with ≥3 conversions and >3x ad-set average → pause candidate.

Winner graduation?
├─ 30-day cohort shows bookings at acceptable cost per booking → Duplicate into scaling structure; re-measure. Do NOT merge into the winner's ad set (restarts its learning).
└─ Cohort shows clicks but no bookings → The hook earned attention, not customers. Keep it as organic content; don't scale it as an ad.

Declining campaign?
├─ Check budget changes → delivery → CTR → form → CRM → quality → tracking → seasonality (§5).
├─ Cause found post-click → Fix the post-click step (form, follow-up, tracking), not the creative.
├─ Cause is creative (CTR/frequency/CPM moved) → Refresh in a separate ad set; keep the old ad running until the replacement proves itself.
└─ No cause found + small sample → Wait out the pre-committed window. Do not pause on a 4-lead week.
```

All five carry the same close: the service, the place, the price ($60 under 50 lb / $75 over, no hidden fees), and "Amanda will text you back."

1. **Review proof (tests whether social proof in the first 3 seconds earns cheaper clicks).** Opening visual: Amanda holding a phone showing Google reviews. Why: the press-clipping reviews creative is the account's only proven volume winner. Trial signal to beat: sends-per-reach + watch time above trial median. 2. **Shy-dog empathy (tests whether an anxiety-first opening qualifies better — fewer, better leads).** Opening visual: a shy dog hanging back while others play. First words (proposed): "Shy dog? 3. **First-night fear (tests whether naming the hardest night converts planners).** Opening visual: Amanda settling a dog into a home bed at night. ## 11. Keep / revise / pause criteria (with small-sample and booking-delay caveats)

**Floors before any verdict:** an ad under 7 days old = watch, always. An ad with <$20 spend or <1,000 impressions = no verdict on reach signals; <100–200 clicks = no verdict on landing/form behavior. A campaign under 30 days old = no revenue verdict — judge delivery and qualified conversations only.

**Keep running:** delivery healthy (impressions pacing with budget, CTR at or above the ad set's median), clicks arriving, qualified conversations arriving at any rate — even if CPL looks high in week one. Never pause the top-converting ad in an ad set.

**Revise (change ONE element, then re-test):** strong opening (low 3-second skip) but weak retention/sends → the body or payoff is the problem, not the hook. Strong retention but weak sends → watchable but not shareable/actionable → change the offer, CTA, or closing, not the hook. CTR healthy but click→lead weak → check the form (length, questions, pre-fill, mobile rendering) before rewriting the ad.

**Pause candidates [Practitioner opinion heuristics, not rules]:** 0 conversions with spend > 2x the ad-set CPA target; CPA > 3x the ad-set average WITH ≥ 3 conversions. 1–2 conversions at high CPA = watch, insufficient data. Zero impressions after 48 hours = investigate (overlap, budget, audience size, rejection) — not a creative verdict.

**Booking-delay caveats (apply to every verdict):** judge a lead cohort, not a lead day. A November ad's bookings arrive over the following 30 days; a lead for a December trip is not a November failure. Never pause a campaign on a 4-lead week. Never declare a winner on CPL before the 30-day cohort shows bookings. Never compare a 7-day-old campaign's revenue to a 6-month-old campaign's revenue.

**Trial-reel decisions (72h, relative to your own trial median):** Paid test = sends-per-reach AND average watch time both at/above median, AND the reel carries (or can carry without changing the hook) a local, bookable offer + lead-compatible CTA. Iterate = strong opening but weak retention/sends (change one element). Drop = below median on both retention and sends at 72h with no profile visits/follows. Never decide on views alone; never compare a trial to a normal reel.

## 13. Reusable research process (use for every future question)

1. **Verify in Ads Manager first.** Campaign names, remembered budgets, and remembered settings are unreliable — this account proved it three times in one pull. Check the Budget column, Budget strategy, optimization event, attribution setting, and form before recommending anything.
2. **Pull the full account, not a sample.** Never call a pull "complete" until a full-account check backs the word — the Oct 22 creative pull missed the 10th image until challenged, and this review exists because a partial pull was presented as all.
3. **Separate the four buckets in every finding:** verified account facts / Meta guidance / practitioner opinion / hypotheses. If a claim has no bucket, it does not go in the report.
4. **Check post-click before blaming creative.** Delivery metrics first (impressions, reach, frequency, CTR, CPM, CPC), then form, then CRM, then quality, then seasonality. A click→lead break is not a creative failure.
5. **Cohort, don't day-judge.** Leads, qualified conversations, bookings, deposits, and collected cash are different numbers on different timelines. Report all five, never one as a proxy for another.
6. **Log decisions with dates.** Every pause, budget change, launch, and verdict gets a date and a reason — because the next diagnosis will need to know what changed when.
7. **Re-verify after any change.** A recommendation's assumptions (budgets, settings, dataset health) expire the moment someone edits the account. Re-pull before re-using a number.
