# Playing a bigger game: the 3x math (Nov 1, 2026)

Alex: "I've spent the same amount on Meta since day one. My goal is 3x revenue. Do I need to play a bigger game?"
Answer: yes. But the data says the multiplier is lead to booking, then budget.

## Data (GHL opportunities via Windsor, created Aug to Oct 2026)
- 142 won bookings in 3 months. Average booking value: **$424**.
- Won by source: Google (Google, Google Ads, Google Organic) 59; Meta (Meta, Facebook Lead Form, Facebook, Meta Ads) 38; social media 13; other 32. **Google brings more bookings than Meta.** Google spend is not connected to Windsor, so its cost is unknown.
- Meta leads that became tracked bookings, by month created:

| Month | Meta leads | Won | Booked value | Meta spend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug | 80 | 11 (14%) | $6,080 | not pulled |
| Sep | 66 | 5 (8%) | $1,430 | not pulled |
| Oct | 83 | 4 (5%) | $1,535 | $2,130 |

- Caveats: 128 Facebook Lead Form opportunities are still open (many are holiday dates); some Meta bookings get relabeled at won; repeat stays are not counted. True Meta return is higher than shown, but nobody can prove by how much.

## The 3x math
- Revenue about $30K a month. 3x is about $90K: about 140 more bookings a month at $424.
- 60 dogs at about $65 a day full = about $117K a month. 3x needs about 77% average occupancy. Capacity is there.
- Meta leads cost about $22. Ad spend needed for 140 extra bookings:

| Lead to booking rate | Leads needed | Meta spend a month |
|---|---|---|
| 5% (October) | 2,800 | about $62,000 |
| 10% | 1,400 | about $31,000 |
| 20% | 700 | about $15,400 |

Close rate is the multiplier on the whole game. At 5%, more budget burns cash. At 20%, $15K a month buys 3x.

## The plan
1. **Measure cost per booking, not cost per lead.** Every won booking keeps its first source tag (Codex in GHL).
2. **Work the 128 open Meta leads now.** Holiday date reactivation, drafted for Alex to send by hand. Free revenue already paid for.
3. **Lift lead to booking from 5% toward 20%.** Reply within 5 minutes, the sales order, a day 1, 3 and 7 follow up.
4. **Scale rule:** while Meta cost per booking stays at or under **$100** (about a quarter of a $424 booking), raise budget 20 to 30% every 3 days, Alex's yes each step. No ceiling but that number and capacity.
5. **Look at Google.** It already wins the most bookings. Connect Google Ads to Windsor so we can see its cost per booking.

## Alex's correction (Nov 1): Meta is cold, Google is searching
- Google catches people already searching for a sitter (demand capture). Meta shows up in feeds of people who weren't looking (demand creation). Meta leads take longer to book.
- The data agrees: the oldest cohort (August, 3 months old) is Meta's best at $79 a booking. Newer cohorts haven't had time.
- New rule: judge Google on bookings in 30 days; judge Meta on bookings from each month's leads after 60 and 90 days (cohort cost per booking). Recheck October's Meta cohort on Dec 31.
- Why Meta still matters for 3x: Google is capped by how many people in Broward search each month. Meta is how we reach past that cap. Meta only pays off with follow up: the 128 open leads, plus a day 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 sequence.

## Google impression share (Windsor, last 30 days, pulled Nov 1)
Campaign "South Florida's Trusted Pet Boarding": spend $2,642.85, 6,162 impressions, 416 clicks (6.8% CTR, $6.35 per click), 52 Google conversions ($50.82 each).
- Search impression share: **10.7%**. We show for about 1 in 9 eligible searches.
- Lost to budget: **80.9%**. Lost to rank: 12.5%.
- Read: when we show, we rank well (Alex: number 1 in 30 miles). The budget runs out before most searches happen. More budget buys more searches. Roughly 57,000 eligible impressions a month vs our 6,162.
- Expect cost per click to rise as budget rises, so scale in steps and judge cost per booking in GHL (October $111, ceiling $150).
- Free alternative: tighten the 30 mile radius so the same $3,000 covers the closest searchers fully.

## Google search terms audit (Windsor, last 30 days, Nov 1)
The campaign "South Florida's Trusted Pet Boarding" is **Performance Max**, not plain Search. Of $2,643 spent, $1,825 shows search terms; the other about $818 ran on Maps, YouTube, Display and Gmail.

| Search type | Spend | Share | Clicks | Google "conversions" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pet stores (Petco, PetSmart, pet shops, botanicas) | $522 | 29% | 56 | 8 |
| Boarding, sitter, kennel, dog hotel | $377 | 21% | 94 | 6.5 |
| Shelters, adoption, surrender my dog | $343 | 19% | 47 | 7 |
| Competitor names (Pet Paradise, Country Inn, Hounds Town, Central Bark) | $260 | 14% | 27 | 6 |
| Groomers, vets, pet spas | $206 | 11% | 22 | 4 |
| Four Paws Inn by name | $69 | 4% | 27 | 1 |
| Other | $48 | 3% | 18 | 0 |

- About **$1,070 a month (59% of search spend)** went to people looking for a pet store, a shelter, a vet or a groomer. Only about 35% went to boarding searches and competitor names.
- Google counts "conversions" on searches like "where can I surrender my dog", so its 52 conversions are not bookings. Its conversion goal needs checking.
- So the 81% "lost to budget" is a share of all these searches, junk included. It does NOT mean 8 of 10 boarding leads are lost.
- Fix before scaling: negative keywords for stores, shelters, adoption, vets and groomers (Windsor push_negative_keywords, campaign level, reversible).
