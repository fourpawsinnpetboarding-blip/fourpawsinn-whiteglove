## 2. What the numbers say
1. **Watch time drives everything.** The 4 reels people watched for 7 to 12 seconds got 2 to 3x the views. Most reels lose people by second 3 to 5. The hook is the problem, not the algorithm.
2. **The winners ask the question a nervous dog parent asks.** "Compare what's included", "what your dog sees on day one", "how we customize the stay". Pretty dog moments with no question ("two dogs, a patch of grass") get watched for 3 seconds.
3. **Statics get almost nothing.** 34 statics: 3 comments, 3 shares. Reels got 14x the shares.
4. **We post 5x more than peers, and each post is weaker.** Instagram shows a new post to a small slice of followers first. When most posts get no reaction, Meta learns our followers don't care and shows the next one to fewer people.
5. **Shares are the growth lever.** One share puts the reel in front of a dog parent's friends, who are local. Our best reels earn 13 shares. That is the number to grow.

## 3. The ratchet (same idea as content/video/baseline-system.md, for organic)
Every post is scored 48 hours after it goes up:
- **Hold:** average watch seconds (reels), or reach vs followers (statics).
- **Spread:** shares + saves + comments per 1,000 views.
- **Baseline:** the best reel of the last 30 days on each. Today: 12.5 s watch, 18.5 per 1,000 ("compare what's included").

Weekly loop (Monday, with the weekly-strategist run):
1. Rank last week's posts on Hold and Spread.
2. For the top post, write one line: **why it won** (the hook type, first frame, length, topic).
3. Next week, 3 posts reuse that winning element with a new topic. One element changes at a time.
4. A post that beats the baseline becomes the new baseline. The loser's idea goes on the "tried, lost" list and isn't repeated.

First 3 second rules (every reel):
- First frame: a real dog in motion or a face, never a logo, a slow pan or text on a blank card.
- First line on screen: a question a nervous dog parent actually asks, or a surprising fact ("Most boarding charges extra for play time").
- Captions burned in, readable with the sound off.

## 6. Test hooks before posting (Adley Kinsman reel, Alex sent Oct 23, 2026)
Idea: never guess which hook is best. Make 2 or 3 versions of the same reel (different first 3 seconds), test them cheaply, read the retention graph (the line showing where viewers leave), fix the weak spot, then post the winner.

How we do it:
1. **Trial Reels (free, built into Instagram).** A trial reel is shown only to non-followers first. Each noon reel gets 2 trial versions with different hooks 48 hours before its slot. Codex posts them from the Instagram app or Meta Business Suite (Eden can't post trials), then reads each one's retention graph and average watch time at 24 hours.
2. **The winner goes to the noon slot.** The losing hook goes on the "tried, lost" list in hook-lab.md.
3. **Optional paid check ($5 dark post):** an ad shown only to the audience, never on the page. $5 per version gets about 1,000 views and a retention graph. Needs Alex's yes because it is spend.
4. **What to fix:** the second where the graph drops hardest. Under 3 s = hook. Middle drop = a slow or confusing shot. End drop = the call to action came too late or a still card.
5. **Goal:** 50%+ of viewers still watching at the halfway point.
