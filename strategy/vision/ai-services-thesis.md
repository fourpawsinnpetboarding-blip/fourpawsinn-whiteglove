## 1. The short version

1. Four Paws Inn is the engine. It pays the bills and it is the proof. Get it to $60K, then $100K. Nothing below changes that order.
2. In 12 to 24 months, AI makes "doing marketing work" nearly free. Anyone selling hours of setup, ads management, or content editing gets squeezed.
3. What gets *more* valuable when AI is everywhere: trust, a real audience, real footage, proof, owned data, and physical businesses that care for animals. AI cannot hold a leash.
4. So we do not build an agency. We build three layers: an audience (now), a productized operating system for pet businesses (2027, when the triggers hit), and ownership of more pet businesses (2028+).
5. Your job shifts from doing the work to directing agents, telling the story, selling the offer, and doing deals.

## 3. What we build: three layers

### Layer 1: Audience and proof (now, 2 hours a week)
- Founder's Log on Substack, YouTube, X. Diary voice. Real numbers when Alex approves them.
- Every win at FPI becomes a story. Every story builds the list of pet business owners who will buy Layer 2.
- Owned list over rented followers: Substack emails and phone numbers are ours. Followers belong to the platform.

### Layer 2: The Pet Business Operating System (start when the launch rule hits, likely 2027)
The exact stack FPI runs, packaged:
1. AI first reply (the 90 second personal text, sociability branches).
2. Booking sheet to CRM sync, so the owner types one number.
3. CAPI tracking, so ads learn who books.
4. Review engine and post stay referral (auto checkout).
5. Money model templates: Pack Member, Board and Train, Holiday Package.
6. Content engine: Claude skills for reels, Founder's Log style posts, the 7:30 AM and 12 PM slots.

Delivery: GHL SaaS mode snapshot (a copy of every workflow, funnel and template, resold under our brand), plus the written SOPs from this repo.

Pricing path:
1. First 10 owners: paid pilot, setup fee plus monthly. Learn what breaks.
2. Then: monthly subscription (staircase math: 300 owners x $497 = about $149K a month).
3. Later: performance pricing (a fee per booked stay we can track). Only once our tracking is proven across 10+ businesses.

Who first: boarding, daycare and grooming owners. Same customer, same problems, same software.
Who later: veterinarians. Reasons to wait: long sales cycles, compliance, locked in PIMS (practice management software that runs the clinic), and big corporate groups buying up clinics. Treat vets as referral partners for now, not customers.

Launch rule (unchanged, from `content/founders-log/README.md`): FPI at $60K a month for 3 months in a row, Board and Train at 10 a month, and 50+ owners raising their hand.

### Layer 3: Own more dirt (2028+)
- Buy pet boarding businesses from retiring owners, install the operating system, raise margin. See `strategy/acquisitions/`.
- Valuation basics: small businesses sell at a multiple of SDE (seller's discretionary earnings: profit plus the owner's pay and perks). If we buy at about 2.5 to 3x SDE and our system lifts SDE by a third, the value we created is ours.
- Financing: SBA 7(a) loans (bank loans partly guaranteed by the Small Business Administration) are the usual tool. Learn this in 2027, use it in 2028.

### The data moat (runs under all three)
Every business on our system reports the same numbers: leads, reply rate, booked rate, revenue per dog, holiday fill. In 2 years we hold benchmarks no one else has. That becomes:
1. A free yearly "State of Pet Boarding" report (lead magnet and authority).
2. The proof behind performance pricing.
3. Due diligence for acquisitions: we know what a good business looks like in numbers.

Privacy rule: aggregate only, never sell or share any client's personal data.

## 5. Getting ready for AI that is not public yet

1. **Write everything down.** Every SOP in this repo is readable by today's agents and tomorrow's. When a better agent arrives, it plugs into the manual on day one. The repo is the asset.
2. **Keep data clean and in one place.** Every booking tagged with a source. We just fixed this.
3. **Own the relationship.** Phone numbers, emails, Substack subscribers. Not platforms.
4. **Build a real footage library.** Real dogs, real rooms, real people. This gets more valuable as fake content floods.
5. **Quarterly model test.** Once a quarter, run the newest AI model on our hardest task (a lead reply, a video edit, a report). Switch when it beats the current way. Otherwise ignore the hype.
6. **Keep fixed costs flexible.** No long software contracts. Tools will change fast.
