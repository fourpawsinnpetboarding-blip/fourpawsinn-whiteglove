## Math check on the constraints

1. **Owner hour value.** 6 hours/week is about 26 hours/month. $100K / 26 = about $3,850 of revenue per owner hour. Only two model types clear that: (a) high ticket offers where someone else delivers, or (b) licensing, royalty, or rev share where nobody delivers hourly.
2. **Margin vs delegation.** 70% margin rules out most delegated services. Contract delivery usually costs 30% to 50% of revenue. Labor heavy acquisitions typically run 15% to 30% margins. So the 70% margin and the 6 hour cap pull against each other.
3. **Capital.** $10K funds a 30 day demand test. It does not fund a build or a down payment. SBA appetite exists, but acquisitions conflict with the 70% margin requirement.
4. **Floor gap.** Four Paws Inn profit is about $20K/month. The floor is $30K/month. Either other income exists or there is already a $10K gap.
5. **Proof standard.** "$0 to $30K in 8 months" is a strong signal only if the unit economics hold: spend, CAC, inquiry to booking rate, and how much came from paid vs reviews and referrals.

## Open questions (Round 2)

1. Does the $100K include Four Paws Inn's $30K, or is it new revenue on top? What does the $30K floor measure: household take-home after tax, or business profit?
2. Why 6 hours? What fills the rest of the week, and would hours move to 15 if a test proves demand by day 30?
3. The $0 to $30K proof: total ad spend, new clients, CAC, inquiry to booking rate, paid vs organic split, current occupancy.
4. Top 3 workflows: what each does, hours saved or revenue produced, who built it.
5. Network, licenses, owned assets, energizing vs draining work, build vs buy vs partner.
