# Deep work operating system

Alex's personal execution system: the daily schedule, the health/family non-negotiables, and the Queue Protocol that solves the actual bottleneck (knowing what to do the second a deep work block starts, and never stalling on a blocker). Built 2026-10-02. This is a personal system, not Four Paws Inn client-facing content, but it lives here because it is the thing that runs the content machine, the lead flow, and the ad scaling described below.

---

## 1. The real problem, named precisely

Not "I don't have a schedule." The schedule was never the bottleneck. The bottleneck, in Alex's own words: sitting down for a 90 minute block and being "kinda knowing, kinda not knowing" what to do, moving too fast through a vague task, hitting a snag, and not having a clear next move, so the block dies.

A calendar cannot fix that. Only a pre-built, oversized queue of concrete next actions can, because it removes every decision from inside the work block. Deciding what to do and doing the work are two different cognitive modes. Mixing them inside a 90 minute window is what was failing.

---

## 2. The Queue Protocol (the formula)

Four moves, always in this order: **LOAD -> RUN -> PARK -> STOP**, plus one **LOG** at day's end.

### LOAD (night before, 15 minutes, never during a work block)

Pick one **Mission**: a single outcome sized at 4 to 12 Deep Work Units (a DWU is one 90 minute block), meaning it should survive 2 to 4 days before running out. Undersized missions are what caused the original failure, running out of task mid-block and having to decide live.

Break the Mission into a literal, numbered checklist. Every line must be something that can be started within 10 seconds of reading it, no interpretation required. "Edit Reel 1: cut to 30s, add captions, export" is a line. "Work on Reels" is not.

Rotate Missions across the four things that actually move the business right now, so no single lever starves the others for weeks straight:

1. **Content production** (filming prep, editing, the reel template, the morning carousel queue)
2. **Lead flow and nurture copy** (GHL sequences, SMS/email copy, call scripts)
3. **Meta ad creative and spend** (new creative batches, the weekly ad review per `strategy/meta-ad-review-playbook.md`)
4. **Finding and onboarding an editor or content director** (the explicit goal to get Alex out of the edit seat)

### RUN (the 90 minute block itself)

Timer on. Open the Queue. Start at the first unchecked line. Work top to bottom. The list already decided what's next, so there is no "what should I do now" moment inside the block, ever.

### PARK (when a line can't be finished)

Max 2 minutes attempting the obvious unblock. Still stuck: write one line under PARKED naming the blocker and what would unblock it, then move immediately to the next queue line. A stuck item skips. A stuck item never ends the session.

### STOP (hard stop at 90 minutes, regardless of progress)

Write one line: **RESUME HERE: [exact next action]**. This single line is what kills the "re-deciding" problem at the start of the next block. The next session's RUN step starts by reading this line, not by figuring out where things stand.

### LOG (end of day, 5 minutes)

Move completed lines to a done list. Note what carries to tomorrow. If the Mission is exhausted, LOAD the next one now, not in the morning. Deciding the next day's work the night before is the actual goal; the Mission sizing above is what makes that possible.

## 3. Daily schedule

One fixed skeleton, same every weekday. Wake anchor: **7:00 AM**, inside the stated ideal window (6:30 to 8:00). Consistency matters more than which exact time in that window gets picked.

| Time | Block | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00 - 7:15 | Wake + 2 minute ritual | Water, read the Queue's RESUME HERE line. No new decisions this early. |
| 7:15 - 8:45 | Deep Work 1 | Protected. Highest-leverage Mission work (content or lead flow). Phone away. |
| 8:45 - 10:15 | Break 1 | Workout here: yoga, run, strength, boxing, or tennis. 60 minutes, real break. |
| 10:15 - 11:45 | Deep Work 2 | Protected. Second highest-leverage block. |
| 11:45 - 13:15 | Break 2 | Lunch + reading (30-60 min fits here). |
| 13:15 - 14:45 | Deep Work 3 | **Deliberately the flexible-tier block** - see section 4. |
| 14:45 - 19:00 | Family / logistics | Tue, Thu, Fri: family and flexible overflow. Mon, Wed: solo pickup and drop-off, Amanda at cheerleading 2-7pm. |
| 19:00 - 21:30 | Family | Dinner and together time. This is the block that has to be protected, phone off, especially Mon/Wed since the afternoon wasn't real family time those two days. |
| 21:30 - 23:30 | Wind-down | Reading if not done earlier. Queue LOAD for tomorrow, 15 minutes, not optional. Screens off by 23:30. |
| 00:00 | Lights out | Gives 7 hours to a 7:00 wake. Workable, not generous - moving lights-out to 23:30 buys 7.5 hours for the same wake time, worth trying if 7 hours doesn't hold up after a couple weeks. |

**The math, shown plainly:** three 90 minute blocks plus two 90 minute breaks between them is 7.5 hours, start to finish. Starting at 7:15 puts the end of Deep Work 3 at 14:45, which still lands after the kids get home around 2:00. There is no schedule that fits this exact structure and finishes before 2:00 without waking earlier than the stated comfortable range - so block 3 is designed around the interruption instead of pretending it won't happen.

### Mon/Wed specific note

Amanda gone 2-7pm means Alex solo-covers pickups and drop-offs those two afternoons. That window is not family time and not deep work time, it is logistics - plan it as such rather than feeling behind when nothing else gets done in it. The 7-9:30pm block becomes the main family anchor on these two days and should be protected harder than on Tue/Thu/Fri, where the afternoon already carries some of the family-time load.

## 4. Why Deep Work 3 is the "flexible tier" block, on purpose

Kids are home by 2:00 most days. Deep Work 3 runs 13:15-14:45, so the back end of it will get interrupted most days, regardless of schedule discipline. Fighting that is wasted energy.

The fix: match task difficulty to how protected the block actually is.

- **Deep Work 1 and 2** (mornings, fully protected): the hardest, highest-leverage Mission work. Scripting and editing a case study reel, writing a nurture sequence, building ad creative variants. Work that needs real, uninterrupted focus.
- **Deep Work 3** (early afternoon, interruption-prone): lower-cognitive-load Mission work that survives being picked up and dropped. Reviewing and approving drafts, replying to leads, light edit passes, admin, scheduling.

This is not a downgrade. It is designing around a real constraint instead of losing a block every time a kid walks in the door.

## 5. Non-negotiables, tracked daily

- Workout: 60+ minutes (Break 1)
- Reading: 30-60 minutes (Break 2 or wind-down)
- Family: 3+ hours with Amanda and the kids (evening block, more on Tue/Thu/Fri)
- Sleep: lights out by midnight, wake 7:00 AM

## 6. The scorecard

Two layers, because ritual adherence and business results are different questions and both matter.

**Daily ritual scorecard:** wake time hit, all 3 Deep Work blocks run, workout done, reading minutes, family hours, bedtime hit, RESUME HERE line written at every stop.

**Weekly business scorecard**, tied to the flywheel Alex described - content volume and ad spend feed lead flow, lead flow feeds data, data improves nurture and copy, better nurture and copy improves conversion, and the cycle repeats: content pieces published, ad creatives added, ad spend, leads generated, nurture touches sent, bookings closed.

The live, trackable version of both lives in the Deep Work Formula tool (artifact link given in chat). This file is the system of record for the logic; the tool is where the daily numbers actually get logged.
